The line is uniform(keccak(R, drawId, you, tier), totalWeight × k), computable by anyone, for any address, in any draw. An observer holds one half of that comparison and can never obtain the other. This is PoolTogether V5's own construction, and it is the only shape of the problem that survives encryption.
    THE INSIGHT

    There is no claim step, and that is a security property.

    Anyone computes their own result
    The threshold is public and you know your own deposits. No transaction needed.
    →
    So a loser never claims
    Why pay gas for zero? Only winners would bother.
    →
    “Who claimed” becomes “who won”
    The leak returns on the cheapest observation available.
    So accrual is unconditional
    A keeper runs accrue(user, draw) for every participant, winner or loser — identical transactions differing only in an encrypted comparison. Nobody has to act to be paid, so acting reveals nothing.
    PoolTogether removed the user's burden. We removed the signal.
    BUT CLAIM EXISTS There is a claim(address) and it has moved real cUSDC on Sepolia — it just does not mean what the word means everywhere else. Your winnings are credited by accrue whether or not you do anything; claim only moves them into your spendable balance, and a deposit or a withdrawal does the same thing on the way past. It is optional, it is permissionless — anyone may call it for anyone — and it behaves identically whether or not you won, which is exactly why it is safe to have at all.

    THE LEAK WE FOUND IN OUR OWN POOL

    We turned our measurement technique on ourselves, and it worked.

    Not a closed bug and not somebody else's. Live on this deployment, and the reason the aggregate is the honest part of the boundary rather than the safe part.

    totalWeight ₓ

    Deposits and withdrawals are public events with plaintext timestamps, and totalWeight is published once per draw. One unknown, one equation — so a window containing exactly one balance change solves rather than bounds.

    An incomplete event filter returns clean integers that are confidently wrong about real people. Almost right is indistinguishable from right — which is why the naive version of this attack is more dangerous than the correct one.
    Measured, not asserted: 12 balance-changing events, 1 solved exactly. It needs a window with a single mover, and at six depositors that is uncommon rather than typical — nothing in the contract enforces a minimum anonymity set, so the frequency is a property of how busy the pool is. Encrypting totalWeight costs 8.3× and destroys the public audit; a randomised house-weight pad in openDraw costs ~0.98M HCU against 17.8M spare and is priced in expected prize value. Both are costed and neither is shipped.
